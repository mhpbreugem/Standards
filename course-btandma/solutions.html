Exercise 4.1: Income Tax

You're given the gross annual salary of an employee in the Netherlands. Assume: the income is only from employment (Box 1) — no income from Box 2 or Box 3; use 2025 tax brackets and tax credit rules; assume a flat employer cost mark-up of 30%; ignore deductions (e.g., mortgage interest, healthcare allowance, etc.). For each salary level, calculate the total income tax owed after applying tax credits, the net income, and the total cost to the employer.

Case A: Gross Salary = €25,000

  • Tax before credits: €25,000 × 35.82% = €8,955
  • Tax credits:
    • General tax credit: full amount = €3,068 (income < €28,406)
    • Labor tax credit: 5.62% of salary → €25,000 × 5.62% = €1,405
  • Tax owed: €8,955 − (€3,068 + €1,405) = €4,482
  • Net income: €25,000 − €4,482 = €20,518
  • Employer cost: €25,000 × 1.30 = €32,500

Case B: Gross Salary = €50,000

  • Tax before credits:
    • First €38,441 × 35.82% = €13,785
    • Remaining €11,559 × 37.48% = €4,333
    • Total = €18,118
  • Tax credits:
    • General: income exceeds €28,406 by €21,594; reduction = €21,594 × 6.337% = €1,364 → €3,068 − €1,364 = €1,704
    • Labor: income above €43,071 by €6,929; reduction = €6,929 × 6.51% = €451 → €5,599 − €451 = €5,148
  • Tax owed: €18,118 − (€1,704 + €5,148) = €11,266
  • Net income: €50,000 − €11,266 = €38,734
  • Employer cost: €50,000 × 1.30 = €65,000

Case C: Gross Salary = €100,000

  • Tax before credits:
    • First €38,441 × 35.82% = €13,785
    • Next €38,376 × 37.48% = €14,379
    • Remaining €23,183 × 49.5% = €11,470
    • Total = €39,634
  • Tax credits:
    • General: fully phased out (income > €76,817) → €0
    • Labor: income above €43,071 by €56,929; reduction = €56,929 × 6.51% = €3,709 → €5,599 − €3,709 = €1,890
  • Tax owed: €39,634 − €1,890 = €37,744
  • Net income: €100,000 − €37,744 = €62,256
  • Employer cost: €100,000 × 1.30 = €130,000

Exercise 4.2: ZZP Net Income

You are a ZZP'er who invoices €100,000 (including 21% VAT) in a year. You meet the hours criterion. You have no significant business expenses. What is your approximate net income?

  • Invoice incl. VAT: €100,000 → revenue excl. VAT: €100,000 ÷ 1.21 = €82,645
  • Deductions:
    • Zelfstandigenaftrek: €5,030 → €82,645 − €5,030 = €77,615
    • MKB-winstvrijstelling (12.7%): €77,615 × 12.7% = €9,857 → €77,615 − €9,857 = €67,758 taxable income
  • Income tax:
    • First €38,441 @ 36.97% ≈ €14,216
    • Remaining €28,917 @ 39.5% ≈ €11,422
    • Subtotal tax: €25,638
  • Tax credits:
    • General tax credit: max €3,070, phases out from €28,406 → gone at €76,837; at €67,758 → credit ≈ €575
    • Labor tax credit: max €5,598, phases out after €43,071 at 6.51% (phase-out: (€67,758 − €43,071) × 6.51% ≈ €1,566) → ≈ €3,991
    • Total credits: €575 + €3,991 = €4,566
  • Net income tax: €25,611 − €4,566 = €21,045
  • Zvw (healthcare contribution): rate 5.32%, max base €71,628; your base €67,758 → €67,758 × 5.32% = €3,605
  • Final net income: €82,645 − €21,045 − €3,605 = €57,995

Exercise 4.3: BV Net Income

You own a BV that invoices €100,000 (including 21% VAT) in a year. The company has no debt and pays all profits as dividends. Assume you pay a DGA salary of €56,000 with only 5.32% Zvw (no other social security) paid as DGA. For simplicity, assume there are no significant costs to run a BV. What is your approximate (personal) net income?

  • Revenue excl. VAT: €100,000 ÷ 1.21 = €82,645
  • BV DGA salary cost: only gross salary €56,000 (Zvw is not paid by the BV) → remaining profit in the BV: €82,645 − €56,000 = €26,645
  • Corporate tax (VPB): 19% over €26,645 = €5,062 → profit after tax (for dividend): €26,645 − €5,062 = €21,583
  • Personal income tax on salary (Box 1), on €56,000:
    • First bracket: €38,441 @ 36.97% ≈ €14,218
    • Second bracket: €17,559 @ 39.5% ≈ €6,927
    • Box 1 tax: €21,145
    • Tax credits: general ≈ €1,229 (max €3,070, phased out 6.337% above €28,406) + labor ≈ €3,826 (max €5,598, phased out 6.51% above €43,071) = €5,055
    • Net income tax: €21,145 − €5,055 = €16,090
  • Zvw (DGA pays personally): 5.32% of €56,000 = €2,979
  • Dividend tax (Box 2): net dividend available €21,583; 24.5% of €21,583 = €5,287 → net dividend: €21,583 − €5,287 = €16,296
  • Final net personal income:
    • Net salary: €56,000 − €16,090 − €2,979 = €36,931
    • Net dividend: €16,296
    • Total net income: €36,931 + €16,296 = €53,227

Exercise 4.4: NV Net Income

Consider an NV with 1M shares outstanding and with total yearly invoices of €100M (including 21% VAT). The company has no debt and pays all profits as dividends. Assume that there are fixed costs of running an NV of €2M per year but that the NV has no other significant costs. Moreover, assume the equity value equals 16 times the yearly NOPAT. What is your approximate (personal) net income if you own (1) 1,000 shares, (2) 100,000 shares?

NV revenue & costs:

  • Total revenue: €100M (including 21% VAT) → remove the VAT portion: €100M ÷ 1.21 = €82.64M (actual revenue excluding VAT)
  • Fixed costs: €2M → EBIT: €82.64M − €2M = €80.64M

Corporate income tax (Vpb):

  • First €200,000 at 19%: €200,000 × 19% = €38,000
  • Remaining €80.44M at 25.8%: €80.44M × 25.8% = €20.77M
  • Total Vpb: €38,000 + €20.77M = €20.81M
  • NOPAT: €80.64M − €20.81M = €59.83M

Equity value & dividends:

  • Equity value at a 16× NOPAT multiplier: 16 × €59.83M = €956.8M
  • Share price: €956.8M / 1M = €956.80
  • Dividends paid out as 100% of net profit: €59.83M → dividends per share: €59.83M / 1M = €59.83
  • NB: realized return is 59.83 / 956.80 = 6.2%

Case 1: You Own 1,000 Shares (Box 3)

  • Your share of dividends: 1,000 × €59.83 = €59,830
  • Share value: 1,000 × €956.80 = €956,800 (your share of equity)
  • Tax-free investment: €57,684
  • Fictitious return: 5.88% of (€956,800 − €57,684) = €56,256
  • Box 3 tax: €56,256 × 36% = €20,252
  • Net personal income (from dividends): €59,830 − €20,252 = €39,578

Case 2: You Own 100,000 Shares (Box 2)

  • Your total dividends: 100,000 × €59.83 = €5,983,000
  • Box 2:
    • Bracket 1: up to €67,804 taxed at 24.5% → tax = €67,804 × 24.5% = €16,627.98
    • Remaining income: €5,983,000 − €67,804 = €5,915,196
    • Bracket 2: €5,915,196 taxed at 31% → tax = €5,915,196 × 31% = €1,833,710.76
    • Total Box 2 tax ≈ €1,850,338.74
  • Net personal income from Box 2: €5,983,000 − €1,850,338.74 ≈ €4,132,661
